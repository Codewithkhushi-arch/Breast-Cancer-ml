About Breast Cancer

Understanding breast cancer is the first step toward prevention, early detection, and effective treatment. Knowledge empowers you to make informed decisions about your health.

1 in 8 Women lifetime risk
99% 5-year survival when caught early
2.3M New cases annually worldwide

What is Breast Cancer?

Breast cancer occurs when cells in the breast tissue grow uncontrollably, forming a tumor that can be felt as a lump or seen on imaging tests.

There are different types of breast cancer, with some growing slowly and others spreading quickly. Early detection significantly improves treatment outcomes.

While breast cancer primarily affects women, it can also occur in men, though this is much less common.

Breast Cancer Awareness: Early Signs & Symptoms

Being aware of these warning signs can help with early detection and better treatment outcomes.

  • Lump or Thickening: A new lump in the breast or underarm area that feels different from surrounding tissue.
  • Skin Changes: Dimpling, puckering, or redness of the breast skin, resembling an orange peel texture.
  • Nipple Changes: Inverted nipple, unusual discharge, or changes in nipple appearance or sensation.
  • Size/Shape Changes: Unexplained changes in breast size, shape, or appearance that persist over time.

Risk Factors

While having risk factors doesn't mean you'll develop breast cancer, awareness helps guide prevention strategies.

  1. Age - Risk increases with age, especially after 50
  2. Family history of breast or ovarian cancer
  3. Genetic mutations (BRCA1, BRCA2, and others)
  4. Prolonged exposure to estrogen
  5. Lifestyle factors (alcohol, obesity, lack of exercise)
  6. Previous breast cancer or certain breast conditions

Why Awareness Matters

Early detection through awareness and regular screening can increase survival rates to over 99%. When caught early, breast cancer is highly treatable with various effective treatment options available.
